## Conditions Treated

The Neurology Department manages stroke and cerebrovascular disease including ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke. Epilepsy and seizure disorders receive specialized management with medication and monitoring. Headaches and migraines are evaluated comprehensively for appropriate treatment. Neurodegenerative diseases including Parkinson's disease, Alzheimer's disease, and motor neuron disease are managed with evidence-based approaches. Spine and nerve disorders including peripheral neuropathy and radiculopathy receive specialized care. The department also treats meningitis and encephalitis, multiple sclerosis, myasthenia gravis, neuropathies, dizziness and vertigo, memory disorders, and sleep disorders.

## Common Symptoms

Seek neurological evaluation for sudden onset weakness or numbness affecting one side of the body. Difficulty speaking, slurred speech, or language comprehension problems warrant urgent assessment. Vision changes including sudden vision loss or double vision require immediate evaluation. Severe headache unlike any previous, especially with fever or neck stiffness, needs emergency care. Loss of consciousness, confusion, or altered mental status indicate serious neurological involvement. Recurrent seizures or loss of consciousness episodes require specialist evaluation. Progressive weakness, balance problems, or difficulty walking suggest neurological disease. Tremors, involuntary movements, or muscle rigidity may indicate movement disorders.

---

## When to Consult a Neurologist

Consult a neurologist for sudden neurological symptoms including weakness, numbness, vision changes, or speech difficulty. Recurrent or severe headaches, especially with associated symptoms, require specialist evaluation. History of seizures or blackout episodes needs comprehensive assessment. Balance problems, dizziness, or coordination issues warrant neurological examination. Progressive memory loss or cognitive changes need early evaluation. Tremors, involuntary movements, or muscle twitching should be professionally assessed. Persistent tingling, numbness in hands or feet, or weakness in limbs indicate peripheral nerve involvement. Family history of neurological disease may warrant preventive screening and counseling.

## Stroke Care Services

The Advanced Stroke Care Unit provides comprehensive evaluation and treatment of acute ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke. The department follows strict stroke protocols with rapid imaging and assessment to determine treatment eligibility. Thrombolytic therapy (tissue plasminogen activator) is administered within therapeutic window for eligible patients. Thrombectomy capability for large vessel occlusion is available through coordination with interventional services.

**Acute Stroke Management**:
- Rapid CT or MRI to differentiate ischemic from hemorrhagic stroke
- Blood work and coagulation studies
- ECG and cardiac assessment
- Thrombolytic therapy initiation within 4.5 hours of symptom onset
- Intensive monitoring for complications
- Blood pressure and glucose management

**Post-Stroke Care**:
- Prevention of recurrent events through risk factor modification
- Antiplatelet or anticoagulation therapy initiation
- Rehabilitation planning and physical therapy
- Speech and swallowing therapy when indicated
- Cognitive and emotional support

---

## Epilepsy Management

Epilepsy management includes comprehensive evaluation with EEG monitoring and imaging to identify seizure source. Antiepileptic medication is carefully selected based on seizure type and patient-specific factors. Continuous EEG monitoring is available for treatment-resistant cases. Surgical evaluation is considered for patients with focal seizures unresponsive to medication.

**Treatment Approach**:
- Accurate seizure classification through EEG and imaging
- First-line antiepileptic drug selection
- Medication adjustment based on efficacy and tolerability
- Drug interaction screening
- Monitoring for side effects and drug levels
- Surgical referral when appropriate
- Vagal nerve stimulation consideration for resistant cases

---

## Headache and Migraine Care

Headaches and migraines receive comprehensive evaluation to identify underlying causes. Migraine management includes both acute and preventive therapies tailored to individual needs. Tension headache, cluster headache, and medication overuse headache are distinguished and treated appropriately. Imaging is obtained when necessary to exclude serious pathology.

**Management Strategy**:
- Detailed history characterizing headache features
- Imaging when indicated for secondary causes
- Acute migraine treatment with triptans or other agents
- Preventive therapy with beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, or antiepileptics
- Lifestyle modification and trigger avoidance counseling
- Botulinum toxin for chronic migraine
- Neuromodulation device consideration

---

## Neurodegenerative Disorders

Parkinson's disease management includes dopaminergic medication, deep brain stimulation evaluation, and multidisciplinary care. Alzheimer's disease evaluation and management focuses on cognitive support and family counseling. Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) receives specialized management with multidisciplinary team coordination. Frontotemporal dementia and Lewy body disease receive appropriate diagnostic evaluation and symptom management.

---

## Spine and Nerve Disorders

Peripheral neuropathy from diabetes, chemotherapy, or other causes receives comprehensive evaluation and management. Radiculopathy from disc herniation or stenosis is managed medically initially with consideration for intervention. Guillain-Barré syndrome receives urgent evaluation and immunotherapy. Carpal tunnel and other entrapment neuropathies are diagnosed through NCS and managed conservatively or surgically as appropriate.

## Emergency Neurological Care

The 24x7 emergency department provides rapid evaluation of acute neurological emergencies. Stroke protocol activation occurs immediately upon suspicion. Seizure management with medication and monitoring prevents status epilepticus. Altered consciousness evaluation determines etiology urgently. Intracranial hemorrhage is immediately identified and managed. Spinal cord compression is recognized and treated emergently.

## Preventive Neurological Health

Stroke prevention through blood pressure control, antiplatelet therapy, and lifestyle modification reduces risk significantly. Seizure prevention in at-risk populations through antiepileptic medication prevents initial occurrence. Cognitive decline prevention through brain health activities, cognitive training, and cardiovascular fitness maintains neurological function. Migraine prevention through trigger avoidance and preventive medication reduces burden. Peripheral neuropathy prevention through glucose control in diabetics minimizes complications.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What should I do if I have sudden weakness or speech difficulty?**
A: Seek emergency care immediately. These are stroke warning signs requiring rapid evaluation and treatment.

**Q: How are seizures diagnosed?**
A: EEG monitoring, imaging, and blood tests help identify seizure source and appropriate treatment.

**Q: Can migraines be prevented?**
A: Yes, preventive medications, lifestyle modification, and trigger avoidance significantly reduce migraine frequency.

**Q: What is the prognosis for Parkinson's disease?**
A: With appropriate management, many patients maintain good quality of life for years after diagnosis.

**Q: When is surgery needed for epilepsy?**
A: Surgery is considered when seizures persist despite optimal medication management.

**Q: Can peripheral neuropathy be reversed?**
A: Some types can improve with treating the underlying cause; others require symptom management.

**Q: Is memory loss always Alzheimer's disease?**
A: No, many conditions cause memory problems. Proper evaluation distinguishes various causes.

**Q: What is the window for stroke treatment?**
A: Most effective within 4.5 hours for thrombolytic therapy; later intervention available for some cases.
