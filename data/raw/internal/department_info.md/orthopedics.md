## Conditions Treated

The Orthopedics Department manages a comprehensive range of musculoskeletal conditions including fractures and dislocations, osteoarthritis and degenerative joint disease, rheumatoid arthritis, ligament and meniscus injuries, sports injuries and overuse syndromes, spinal disorders including disc herniation and stenosis, shoulder disorders (rotator cuff tears, impingement), hip dysplasia and FAI, knee injuries (ACL, PCL, meniscus), foot and ankle conditions, pediatric orthopedic conditions, tumor and bone lesions, infection and osteomyelitis, and metabolic bone disease.

## Common Symptoms

Consult an orthopedic specialist for joint pain and stiffness, especially in knees, hips, shoulders, and spine. Swelling and inflammation in joints indicate inflammatory or traumatic conditions. Limited range of motion, weakness, and instability suggest ligamentous or muscular involvement. Acute trauma with severe pain, deformity, or inability to bear weight requires immediate evaluation. Chronic back and neck pain, numbness, tingling, and radiating pain suggest nerve compression.

---

## When to Consult an Orthopedic Specialist

Seek orthopedic consultation for acute injuries including fractures, dislocations, and severe sprains. Chronic joint pain lasting more than six weeks despite conservative management warrants specialist evaluation. Sports injuries affecting performance or causing persistent symptoms need professional assessment. Progressive spine pain with neurological symptoms (numbness, weakness, bowel/bladder changes) requires urgent evaluation. Degenerative conditions causing functional limitation benefit from comprehensive management planning. Pre-participation screening before sports or strenuous activities is recommended for high-risk individuals.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: When should I see an orthopedic surgeon for joint pain?**
A: Consult if pain persists beyond six weeks despite conservative care, significantly limits activities, or follows acute trauma.

**Q: Can I avoid surgery for arthritis?**
A: Many cases respond to physical therapy, medications, and lifestyle modification. Surgery is considered when conservative measures fail.

**Q: How long does recovery take after joint replacement?**
A: Most patients achieve functional recovery within 3-6 months, with continued improvement up to one year.

**Q: Is arthroscopic surgery less invasive?**
A: Yes, arthroscopy uses small incisions and camera guidance, allowing faster recovery compared to open surgery.

**Q: Can I return to sports after ACL reconstruction?**
A: Yes, with appropriate rehabilitation (typically 6-9 months), most athletes return to their sport safely.

**Q: What is the success rate of spine surgery?**
A: Success rates exceed 80-90% for appropriate patient selection and proper surgical technique.

**Q: How can I prevent osteoporosis?**
A: Ensure adequate calcium and vitamin D intake, perform weight-bearing exercise, avoid smoking, and limit alcohol.

**Q: Is MRI necessary for all joint injuries?**
A: Not always. X-rays often suffice for fractures. MRI is valuable for soft tissue and cartilage assessment.
