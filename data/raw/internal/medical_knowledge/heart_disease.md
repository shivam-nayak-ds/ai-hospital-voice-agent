# Lifeline Multi-Speciality Hospital - Heart Disease Knowledge Document

## Overview

Heart disease, also called cardiovascular disease, refers to a group of conditions affecting the heart and blood vessels. It is the leading cause of death worldwide and a major health concern in India, where it affects people at younger ages compared to Western countries. The most common form is coronary artery disease, which involves narrowing or blockage of the blood vessels supplying the heart muscle.

Heart disease encompasses several conditions including coronary artery disease, heart failure, arrhythmias, valvular heart disease, congenital heart defects, and cardiomyopathy. Risk factors develop early in life and progress silently over decades before symptoms appear. Early detection, aggressive risk factor management, and timely treatment can significantly reduce morbidity and mortality. India faces a particularly high burden with cardiovascular disease accounting for a significant proportion of deaths, often occurring a decade earlier than in Western populations.

## Causes

Coronary artery disease is caused by atherosclerosis, a process where fatty deposits called plaque build up inside the coronary arteries over years. This buildup narrows the arteries and reduces blood flow to the heart muscle. When a plaque ruptures, a blood clot forms that can completely block the artery causing a heart attack.

Heart failure occurs when the heart muscle weakens or stiffens and cannot pump blood effectively. Common causes include coronary artery disease, long-standing hypertension, valvular heart disease, and cardiomyopathy. Arrhythmias result from abnormalities in the heart's electrical system caused by heart damage, electrolyte imbalances, or genetic conditions. Valvular heart disease can be caused by rheumatic fever, degenerative changes, congenital defects, or infections.

## Risk Factors

- High blood pressure above 140/90 mmHg
- High cholesterol especially elevated LDL above 100 mg/dL
- Diabetes mellitus with poorly controlled blood sugar
- Smoking and tobacco use in any form
- Family history of premature heart disease in first-degree relatives
- Obesity with BMI above 30 or waist circumference above 90 cm for men and 80 cm for women
- Sedentary lifestyle with minimal physical activity
- Unhealthy diet high in saturated fat, trans fat, salt, and sugar
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Chronic stress and poor sleep quality
- Age above 45 for men and above 55 for women
- Indian and South Asian ethnicity with higher genetic predisposition

## Symptoms

**Coronary Artery Disease Symptoms**: Chest pain or discomfort described as pressure, heaviness, squeezing, or burning in the center or left side of the chest. Pain may radiate to the left arm, neck, jaw, back, or shoulder. Symptoms are typically triggered by exertion and relieved by rest. Shortness of breath during activity may be the only symptom in some patients.

**Heart Attack Symptoms**: Sudden severe chest pain lasting more than 20 minutes not relieved by rest, cold sweating, nausea and vomiting, extreme weakness, shortness of breath, and anxiety. Women may present with atypical symptoms including fatigue, indigestion, and back or jaw pain.

**Heart Failure Symptoms**: Progressive shortness of breath, initially with exertion and later at rest. Orthopnea (difficulty breathing when lying flat), paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (waking up breathless), swelling in legs and ankles, rapid weight gain from fluid retention, fatigue and weakness, and persistent cough.

**Arrhythmia Symptoms**: Palpitations or awareness of heartbeat, skipped beats, racing heart, dizziness, lightheadedness, fainting, chest discomfort, and shortness of breath.

## Diagnosis

Diagnosis begins with a thorough history and physical examination. Electrocardiogram (ECG) records the heart's electrical activity and detects rhythm abnormalities, ischemia, and prior heart attacks. Echocardiography uses ultrasound to assess heart structure, function, valve integrity, and ejection fraction. Stress testing evaluates the heart's response to exercise or pharmacological stress, revealing ischemia not apparent at rest.

Coronary angiography through cardiac catheterization provides definitive diagnosis of coronary artery disease by visualizing the coronary arteries directly. Cardiac CT angiography provides non-invasive coronary assessment. Cardiac MRI offers detailed assessment of myocardial structure and function. Blood tests include lipid profile, blood glucose and HbA1c, cardiac biomarkers including troponin and BNP, and renal function. The Advanced Cath Lab at Lifeline Multi-Speciality Hospital provides comprehensive diagnostic and interventional cardiac services.

## Treatment Options

Treatment depends on the specific condition and severity. Coronary artery disease management includes lifestyle modifications, pharmacological therapy, percutaneous coronary intervention with stent placement through the catheterization lab, and coronary artery bypass grafting surgery for complex disease.

Heart failure treatment includes guideline-directed medical therapy with multiple medications, device therapy including pacemakers and defibrillators, cardiac resynchronization therapy, and advanced therapies including mechanical support and transplantation evaluation.

Arrhythmia management includes antiarrhythmic medications, catheter ablation procedures, pacemaker implantation for bradyarrhythmias, and implantable cardioverter-defibrillator for life-threatening arrhythmias. Valvular heart disease treatment includes medical management, catheter-based interventions including balloon valvuloplasty and transcatheter valve procedures, and surgical valve repair or replacement.

## Medications Commonly Used

- **Antiplatelet Agents (Aspirin, Clopidogrel, Ticagrelor)**: Prevent blood clot formation on stents and in narrowed arteries
- **Statins (Atorvastatin, Rosuvastatin)**: Lower cholesterol and stabilize arterial plaques
- **Beta-Blockers (Metoprolol, Carvedilol)**: Reduce heart rate, blood pressure, and heart workload
- **ACE Inhibitors (Ramipril, Enalapril)**: Lower blood pressure and protect heart function
- **ARBs (Losartan, Telmisartan)**: Alternative to ACE inhibitors with similar benefits
- **Diuretics (Furosemide, Torsemide)**: Remove excess fluid in heart failure
- **Aldosterone Antagonists (Spironolactone)**: Reduce mortality in heart failure
- **Anticoagulants (Warfarin, Apixaban)**: Prevent stroke in atrial fibrillation
- **Nitroglycerin**: Relieves acute angina chest pain

## Lifestyle Management

Adopt a heart-healthy diet emphasizing fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean protein, and healthy fats while limiting saturated fat, trans fat, salt, and sugar. Exercise for at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity. Quit smoking completely and avoid secondhand smoke. Maintain a healthy BMI between 18.5 to 24.9 kg/m². Manage blood pressure below 140/90 mmHg and below 130/80 mmHg for diabetic patients. Control blood sugar with HbA1c below 7%. Limit alcohol intake. Manage stress through yoga, meditation, and adequate sleep. Cardiac rehabilitation after heart events significantly improves outcomes.

## Prevention

Primary prevention targets individuals without known heart disease through risk factor identification and management, healthy lifestyle promotion, regular screening for blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar, and aspirin use only when recommended by a physician. Secondary prevention targets individuals with established heart disease to prevent recurrent events through aggressive risk factor control, medication adherence, lifestyle modifications, cardiac rehabilitation, and regular follow-up.

## Possible Complications

Heart attack causes permanent damage to the heart muscle and can be fatal. Heart failure leads to progressive disability and reduced quality of life. Stroke from atrial fibrillation or carotid artery disease causes brain damage. Peripheral arterial disease can lead to limb ischemia and amputation. Aneurysm formation in weakened blood vessels risks rupture. Sudden cardiac death from fatal arrhythmias. Kidney damage from reduced cardiac output and poor perfusion. Depression and anxiety are common comorbidities affecting recovery.

## When to Seek Medical Care

Visit your physician at Lifeline Multi-Speciality Hospital for routine cardiovascular risk assessment. Seek evaluation for new or worsening chest pain, unexplained shortness of breath, palpitations or irregular heartbeat, swelling in legs or feet, dizziness or fainting episodes, and poor exercise tolerance.

## Emergency Warning Signs

Visit the Emergency Department at Lifeline Multi-Speciality Hospital immediately if you experience crushing chest pain lasting more than 20 minutes, chest pain with cold sweating, nausea, and breathlessness, sudden severe breathlessness, fainting or loss of consciousness, sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body, difficulty speaking, or rapid irregular heartbeat with dizziness. Call for ambulance service if symptoms suggest a heart attack or stroke. The hospital provides 24x7 emergency cardiac care with Advanced Cath Lab availability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can heart disease be reversed?**
A: Early-stage coronary artery disease can be slowed or partially reversed with aggressive lifestyle changes and medications. Advanced disease requires interventions but can still be effectively managed.

**Q: Is chest pain always a heart problem?**
A: No, chest pain can be caused by musculoskeletal issues, acid reflux, anxiety, or lung conditions. However, new or unexplained chest pain should always be evaluated promptly.

**Q: Can young people get heart disease?**
A: Yes, especially in India where heart disease affects younger age groups. Risk factors develop early, and family history plays a significant role.

**Q: How long after a heart attack can I resume normal activities?**
A: Most patients can gradually return to normal activities within 4 to 8 weeks after an uncomplicated heart attack, following cardiac rehabilitation.

**Q: Are stents permanent?**
A: Modern drug-eluting stents are designed to be permanent. Restenosis rates are low with proper medication adherence and lifestyle management.
