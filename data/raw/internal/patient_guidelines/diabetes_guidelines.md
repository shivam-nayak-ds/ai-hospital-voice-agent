# Lifeline Multi-Speciality Hospital - Diabetes Patient Guidelines

## Overview

Diabetes is a long-term condition where your body cannot properly control blood sugar levels. There are two main types. Type 1 diabetes happens when the body stops making insulin. Type 2 diabetes happens when the body does not use insulin properly. Most people have Type 2 diabetes. With proper care, people with diabetes can live healthy and active lives.

## Risk Factors

- Family history of diabetes
- Being overweight or obese
- Age above 45 years
- Sedentary lifestyle with no physical activity
- High blood pressure or high cholesterol
- History of diabetes during pregnancy (gestational diabetes)
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)
- Indian and South Asian ethnicity carries higher risk

## Symptoms

- Frequent urination, especially at night
- Excessive thirst and dry mouth
- Unexplained weight loss
- Increased hunger even after eating
- Blurred vision
- Slow healing of wounds
- Tingling or numbness in hands and feet
- Frequent infections
- Fatigue and low energy

## Prevention

- Maintain a healthy body weight
- Exercise for at least 30 minutes daily
- Eat a balanced diet rich in fiber and low in sugar
- Limit processed food and sugary drinks
- Get regular blood sugar screening after age 45
- Manage stress through relaxation techniques
- Avoid smoking and limit alcohol consumption

## Lifestyle Recommendations

Walk for at least 30 minutes every day. Choose stairs over lifts. Check your blood sugar regularly at home. Maintain a fixed meal schedule. Sleep 7 to 8 hours every night. Manage work stress. Visit your doctor every 3 months for checkups. Keep a record of your blood sugar readings.

## Diet Guidelines

**Eat More**: Whole grains, green vegetables, dal, fruits in moderation, nuts, seeds, curd, and lean protein like fish and chicken.

**Eat Less**: White rice, white bread, sweets, fried food, sugary drinks, fruit juices, processed snacks, and maida products.

**Meal Tips**: Eat small meals 4 to 5 times a day. Never skip breakfast. Avoid eating late at night. Control portion sizes. Choose boiled or grilled food over fried.

## Medication Guidance

- Take medicines exactly as prescribed by your doctor
- Do not skip or stop medicines without consulting your doctor
- Some diabetes medicines need to be taken before meals
- Insulin users should learn proper injection technique
- Store insulin in a refrigerator, not freezer
- Monitor blood sugar as advised by your doctor
- Report any side effects like low blood sugar symptoms immediately

## When to Seek Medical Attention

- Blood sugar readings consistently above 200 mg/dL or below 70 mg/dL
- Wounds that do not heal after one week
- Sudden vision changes
- Chest pain or breathlessness
- Numbness or tingling worsening in feet
- Repeated urinary or skin infections

## Emergency Warning Signs

Seek emergency care at Lifeline Multi-Speciality Hospital if you experience:

- Confusion or difficulty staying awake
- Rapid breathing with fruity-smelling breath
- Severe abdominal pain with nausea and vomiting
- Loss of consciousness or seizures
- Blood sugar below 54 mg/dL with symptoms

These may indicate diabetic ketoacidosis or severe hypoglycemia.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can diabetes be cured?**
A: Type 2 diabetes cannot be fully cured but can be controlled with diet, exercise, and medicines.

**Q: Can I eat fruits if I have diabetes?**
A: Yes, eat fruits in moderation. Choose low-sugar fruits like guava, apple, and berries. Avoid fruit juices.

**Q: How often should I check my sugar?**
A: Follow your doctor's advice. Most patients check fasting and post-meal sugar regularly.

**Q: Is walking enough exercise?**
A: Yes, brisk walking for 30 minutes daily is very helpful for sugar control.
