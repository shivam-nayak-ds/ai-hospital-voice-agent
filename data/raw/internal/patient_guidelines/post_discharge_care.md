# Lifeline Multi-Speciality Hospital - Post Discharge Care Guidelines

## Overview

Good care after leaving the hospital helps you recover faster and prevents complications. These guidelines will help you take care of yourself at home after discharge from Lifeline Multi-Speciality Hospital. Follow all instructions given by your treating doctor and nursing staff.

## Wound Care Instructions

- Keep the surgical wound clean and dry at all times
- Change dressings as instructed by your doctor
- Do not touch the wound with dirty hands
- Watch for signs of infection like redness, swelling, pus, or bad smell
- Do not apply any cream or powder on the wound without doctor advice
- Avoid soaking the wound in water. Take sponge baths until your doctor says it is safe to shower
- Visit the hospital for wound dressing changes as scheduled

## Medication Management

- Take all medicines exactly as prescribed at the same time every day
- Complete the full course of antibiotics even if you feel better
- Do not stop any medicine without asking your doctor
- Use a pill organizer or set phone alarms to remember medicines
- Keep a written list of all your medicines with timing and dosage
- Report any side effects like rash, nausea, or dizziness to your doctor
- Refill medicines before they run out from the 24x7 pharmacy

## Diet and Nutrition After Discharge

- Eat light and easily digestible food for the first few days
- Drink plenty of water and fluids to stay hydrated
- Include protein-rich food like dal, eggs, and paneer for healing
- Eat fresh fruits and vegetables for vitamins and fiber
- Avoid fried, spicy, and heavy food during recovery
- Limit salt and sugar intake
- Avoid alcohol and smoking completely during recovery
- Follow specific dietary instructions given by your doctor

## Activity and Rest

- Get adequate rest and sleep 7 to 8 hours every night
- Start with light walking inside your home
- Gradually increase activity as you feel stronger
- Avoid heavy lifting, bending, or strenuous exercise for the period advised
- Do not drive until your doctor clears you
- Take short walks 2 to 3 times daily to improve blood circulation
- Avoid sitting or standing in one position for too long

## Follow-Up Appointments

- Attend your first follow-up visit as scheduled before discharge
- Bring all discharge papers and investigation reports to follow-up visits
- Get prescribed tests done before your follow-up appointment
- Note down any new symptoms or concerns to discuss with your doctor
- Do not skip follow-up visits even if you feel fine
- The hospital may call you for post-discharge check-in. Please answer these calls

## When to Seek Medical Attention

Contact Lifeline Multi-Speciality Hospital or visit the emergency department if you experience:

- Fever above 101°F that does not go down
- Increasing pain at the surgical site not relieved by medicines
- Redness, swelling, or discharge from the wound
- Nausea or vomiting preventing you from eating or taking medicines
- Difficulty breathing or chest pain
- Swelling in legs or sudden weight gain
- Dizziness or fainting episodes
- Any symptom that feels unusual or worrying

## Emergency Warning Signs

Visit the emergency department immediately if you experience:

- Heavy bleeding from the wound or any body opening
- Severe chest pain or difficulty breathing
- Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body
- Loss of consciousness or confusion
- Severe allergic reaction with swelling and difficulty breathing
- High fever with chills and shaking

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: When can I return to work after discharge?**
A: It depends on your surgery and recovery. Most patients return within 2 to 6 weeks. Ask your doctor.

**Q: Can I climb stairs after surgery?**
A: Limit stair climbing for the first 1 to 2 weeks. Use stairs slowly when necessary.

**Q: When can I take a full bath?**
A: Usually after wound healing, around 10 to 14 days. Confirm with your doctor.

**Q: What if I miss a dose of medicine?**
A: Take it as soon as you remember unless it is almost time for the next dose. Never double the dose.

**Q: How do I get my lab reports after discharge?**
A: Access digital lab reports through the hospital patient portal or collect printed copies from the lab.
